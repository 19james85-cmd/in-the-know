CRITICAL — Active Exploitation BleepingComputer
Gentlemen RaaS deploys custom EDR killers in widescale campaign
The Gentlemen ransomware-as-a-service group — now the second most active RaaS operation by victim count with over 332 claimed victims — has been documented deploying a custom EDR-killing toolkit called GentleKiller with at least eight variants. The tool uses a "bring your own vulnerable driver" (BYOVD) technique to elevate privileges and disable endpoint security engines from Kaspersky, CrowdStrike, and Microsoft. The group targets organizations based on FortiGate firewall configurations and also deploys OxideHarvest, a Rust-based credential stealer, as part of a multi-stage attack chain.
What this means for beginners
Ransomware is malicious software that locks your files and demands payment to unlock them. The Gentlemen group is particularly dangerous because they actively disable the security software — called EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response) — that companies use to catch attackers, making it much harder for victims to detect or stop the attack before their data is encrypted.

Vulnerabilities & CVEs
CVE-2026-20230 — CRITICAL CISA
Cisco Unified Communications Manager SSRF — patch deadline today
CVE-2026-20230 affects Cisco Unified Communications Manager (Unified CM) and Unified CM Session Management Edition. An unauthenticated remote attacker can exploit a server-side request forgery (SSRF) vulnerability to write arbitrary files to the underlying OS, enabling escalation to root. A public proof-of-concept already exists and federal agencies face a patch deadline of June 28, 2026 under CISA's BOD 26-04.
What this means for beginners
A vulnerability is a flaw in software that attackers can exploit — like an unlocked back door into a building. This particular flaw in Cisco's phone system software lets an attacker on the internet write files to the server without logging in, then use those files to gain the highest level of access possible (called "root"). The patch deadline being today means federal agencies must fix this right now.
CVE-2026-34908/09/10 — HIGH CISA
Ubiquiti UniFi OS triple KEV addition — network device cluster at risk
CISA added three Ubiquiti UniFi OS vulnerabilities to the KEV catalog on June 23, 2026: CVE-2026-34908 (improper access control), CVE-2026-34909 (path traversal), and CVE-2026-34910 (improper input validation). Combined, these flaws allow a malicious actor with network access to conduct command injection on widely deployed UniFi networking equipment used in enterprise and home office environments. Mitigations must be applied per BOD 26-04.
What this means for beginners
Ubiquiti UniFi devices are the routers and network switches used in thousands of offices and homes. Three separate vulnerabilities were found in their operating system at once — think of it like discovering the front door, back door, and a window of a building are all unlocked simultaneously. Attackers inside the network can exploit these flaws to run their own commands on the device, giving them control over all traffic flowing through it.
Threat actors & ransomware
DragonForce RaaS BleepingComputer
DragonForce hides C2 traffic inside Microsoft Teams infrastructure
DragonForce ransomware deployed a custom Go-based malware named Backdoor.Turn that abuses Microsoft Teams' TURN relay protocol to disguise command-and-control (C2) traffic as legitimate Teams traffic. The TTPs include obtaining an anonymous Teams visitor token, routing attacker C2 communications through Microsoft's own relay infrastructure, and completing full data exfiltration before deploying ransomware encryption. DragonForce is linked to the Scattered Spider threat group and operates a cartel-style organizational structure. IOCs published by Symantec.
What this means for beginners
Threat actors are hackers or criminal groups who carry out cyberattacks. DragonForce is particularly clever — instead of using obviously suspicious servers to control their malware, they hide their attack traffic inside Microsoft Teams, a tool millions of businesses use every day. Defenders see what looks like normal Teams traffic, so the attack goes undetected until it's too late.
ShinyHunters BleepingComputer
ShinyHunters breaches 100+ organizations via Oracle PeopleSoft zero-days
The ShinyHunters extortion gang has claimed theft of data from over 300 PeopleSoft instances across more than 100 organizations — primarily in the education sector — using a gadget chain of old and zero-day Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerabilities. Known IOCs include staging IPs: 142.11.200[.]186–190, 108.174.202[.]99, and 176.120.22[.]24. MeshCentral agents and credential spray scripts were observed in exposed attacker directories. Victims are receiving extortion demands; Nottingham University confirmed as a named victim.
What this means for beginners
ShinyHunters is a well-known hacking group that steals data and demands money to keep it private. Oracle PeopleSoft is software used by universities and large companies to manage HR, payroll, and student records. By exploiting previously unknown flaws (zero-days), they gained access to sensitive data about employees and students at over 100 organizations — a serious privacy breach affecting potentially millions of people.
Regulatory & compliance
CISA BOD 26-04 CISA
BOD 26-04 enforces three-day patch deadline for max-severity CVEs
CISA's Binding Operational Directive 26-04 — which supersedes BOD 19-02 and BOD 22-01 — establishes new risk-based vulnerability remediation timelines for all Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies. Maximum-severity vulnerabilities (CVSS 10.0) must be patched within 3 days of KEV catalog addition. High-severity vulnerabilities (CVSS 7.0–9.9) must be addressed within deadlines tied to active exploitation evidence. The directive also introduces forensics triage requirements and mandates compliance reporting.
What this means for beginners
CISA is the U.S. government's cybersecurity agency. A "Binding Operational Directive" is a mandatory order all federal government agencies must follow. BOD 26-04 is a new rule that says if a serious vulnerability is discovered and actively being exploited, federal agencies have just 3 days to fix it — down from the previous 15-day window. This reflects how fast attackers move once a vulnerability becomes public.
EU AI Act Phase 2 Cybersecurity Dive
EU AI Act high-risk compliance deadline arrives August 2, 2026
Organizations operating in Europe face mandatory compliance with EU AI Act transparency and governance requirements for high-risk AI systems by August 2, 2026. High-risk categories include AI used in critical infrastructure, employment screening, education, law enforcement, and essential services. The European Commission is finalizing a Code of Practice for AI-generated content labeling. Non-compliance risks enforcement actions from national supervisory authorities across all 27 EU member states, with penalties mirroring GDPR-scale fines.
What this means for beginners
The EU AI Act is a new European law regulating artificial intelligence — think of it as a seatbelt law but for AI systems. If a company uses AI to make decisions about people (like hiring, lending, or healthcare), they must now prove it is safe, transparent, and fair. The August 2026 deadline means companies have just weeks to get compliant or face massive fines similar to those under GDPR, which can reach 4% of global revenue.
Industry trends
AI-Powered Attacks BleepingComputer / Sophos
AI ransomware toolkits now automate EDR evasion and Active Directory discovery
Sophos researchers documented a fully AI-assisted ransomware development workflow where threat actors used Cursor and Claude Opus AI agents to write, test, and refine malware — including EDR bypass payloads tested against Sophos, CrowdStrike, and Microsoft Defender in virtual environments. The workflow is human-directed but AI-accelerated, dramatically compressing the time between offensive security research publication and criminal implementation. Russian-language scripts confirmed actor origin.
What this means for beginners
Artificial intelligence is now being used by criminals to build better malware faster. Just like AI tools help programmers write code more quickly, attackers are using the same technology to write malware that evades detection. This is a significant shift — attacks that used to take skilled hackers weeks to develop can now be built and tested in days, meaning the pace of new threats is accelerating rapidly.
Zero Trust Mandates Dark Reading
Zero trust architecture transitions from best practice to legal requirement in 2026
Following federal executive mandates, zero trust architecture (ZTA) is becoming a regulatory compliance requirement for public sector organizations in 2026 — cascading to state, local, tribal, and territorial (SLTT) governments through updated NIST frameworks, procurement requirements, and cybersecurity grant conditions. Organizations that fail to demonstrate zero trust adoption risk losing federal contracts and grants. The shift is designed to eliminate lateral movement vulnerabilities exposed in campaigns like Salt Typhoon, which compromised over 200 telecommunications providers.
What this means for beginners
"Zero trust" is a security approach that means no one — not even employees inside the building — is automatically trusted. Every user and device must prove their identity every time they try to access something. Previously this was just a recommendation. In 2026, it is becoming the law for government organizations, meaning if you do business with the government and don't implement zero trust, you could lose your contract.
